Behavioral Analytics and Tax Collections

A simple data-informed intervention that made it easier for residents to act and generated more than $1.5 million for Syracuse.

The problem

City government often has plenty of information but not enough useful communication. In this case, the question was whether a small change in how residents received information could change behavior.

What we tried

We used behavioral insights and available data to design a clearer intervention, then measured what happened rather than assuming the message would work.

My role

I helped frame the problem, connect the data to an operational test, and make the result useful to the people responsible for the program.

What happened

The intervention generated more than $1.5 million in additional revenue for Syracuse.

What I learned

Not every public-sector improvement needs a large new system. Sometimes the most valuable work is noticing a small point of friction and being willing to test a better way.
